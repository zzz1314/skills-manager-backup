# Evidence-Grounded Self-Introduction

Read this reference when the user asks for a spoken self-introduction, an
opening pitch, or an answer to "tell me about yourself" for an interview.

## 1. Purpose And Input Boundary

A self-introduction should help the interviewer understand why the candidate
fits the current role and which claims are worth exploring. It should not become
a full biography or a spoken copy of the resume.

Before drafting, identify:

1. Target company, business unit, role, and interview stage.
2. Current JD or another dated role source.
3. Requested speaking time and language.
4. Verified identity facts and two or three relevant experience cards.
5. Company-specific motivation supported by a current primary source.

If the JD, duration, or evidence is missing, state the gap. Produce a reusable
skeleton or a clearly labelled provisional version instead of filling the gap
with generic claims.

Treat external templates, screenshots, social posts, and sample scripts as
method leads. They do not prove the user's background, capability, company
knowledge, or interview standard.

## 2. Four-Part Design

Use four functional parts. They may be delivered as three or four paragraphs.

### Part 1: Identity And Direction

Give the name and only the education, specialization, or current status that
helps the interviewer place the candidate. End with a clear role direction or
one evidence-backed positioning statement.

Do not recite the education section, list personality adjectives, or claim a
professional identity that the evidence does not support.

### Part 2: Relevant Evidence

Select one or two cases with the highest combination of role relevance and
evidence strength. For each case, compress the story into:

`scene + personal task + key judgment or action + result + role relevance`

Spend most of the answer here. Preserve the boundary between personal action
and team result. Use a metric only when its definition, scope, period, and
source can survive follow-up.

Do not list every internship or project. Add the connection the resume cannot
show directly, such as why the action mattered, what judgment guided it, and
how it relates to the target role.

### Part 3: Capability Synthesis

Summarize two or three capabilities already demonstrated by the selected
cases. Each capability must point back to a concrete action or result. Prefer
role language that describes real work, such as customer discovery, content
operation, stakeholder coordination, data handling, or project execution.

Avoid unsupported labels such as strong communication, business sense,
leadership, stress resistance, or fast learning. Replace a label with the
smallest observable proof when possible.

### Part 4: Motivation And Close

Explain why the candidate is choosing this function and why the current role
is a reasonable next step. Add company-specific motivation only when supported
by a dated official source or direct confirmed information. Separate the
source fact from the candidate's interpretation.

Close by naming the value the candidate expects to contribute or by inviting
the interviewer to explore one of the cases. Do not use loyalty promises,
empty enthusiasm, or unverifiable claims about the company.

## 3. Duration Control

Follow the interviewer's stated limit. When no limit is given, prepare a core
version that can usually be delivered in about 60 to 90 seconds and a fuller
version that stays close to two minutes. Treat these as rehearsal targets, not
universal hiring rules.

Prepare three layers:

1. Short fallback: identity, one strongest proof point, and role direction.
2. Core version: identity, one or two evidence modules, capability synthesis,
   and motivation.
3. Follow-up modules: extra detail on scope, judgment, metric, difficulty, or
   reflection, used only when the interviewer asks.

Rehearse with a timer at the candidate's natural speaking pace. Shorten by
removing secondary background and repeated conclusions. Do not speed up until
the answer becomes hard to follow. A script that exceeds the requested time
must be compressed before polishing.

## 4. Drafting Template

Use this as a drafting scaffold. Do not keep the brackets or force every field
into the spoken version.

```text
您好，我是[姓名]，目前[与岗位相关的身份信息]。我这次应聘[岗位]，主要基于[有证据支撑的方向或优势]。

在[经历一]中，我负责[个人任务]。当时的关键问题是[约束或难点]，我通过[关键判断与行动]完成了[交付或结果]。这段经历让我形成了[与岗位工作场景相关的能力]。

[如有必要，再补一段互补经历，避免重复同一种能力。]

结合这些经历，我能为这个岗位带来的核心价值是[能力一]和[能力二]，对应到实际工作中是[具体工作场景]。我选择这个方向，是因为[经核验的职业动机]。对于贵公司的兴趣来自[有日期的官方事实]，我更关注的是[基于事实的个人判断]。以上是我的基本情况，谢谢。
```

When company evidence is unavailable, omit the company-specific sentence and
mark it for research. Do not replace it with a generic compliment.

## 5. Drafting Workflow

1. Extract the JD's repeated responsibilities, work scenes, and hard
   constraints.
2. Rank verified experience cards by relevance, evidence tier, distinctiveness,
   and follow-up defensibility.
3. Choose a primary case and, only when it adds a different capability, one
   supporting case.
4. Write the direct positioning sentence after evidence selection, so the
   opening matches what the answer can prove.
5. Draft the core version, then derive the short fallback and follow-up modules.
6. Read it aloud, time it, and replace written-style phrases with natural
   spoken Chinese without weakening factual boundaries.
7. Run the evidence and follow-up audit below.

## 6. Audit Checklist

Before delivering a final version, verify:

1. The opening identifies the target role and does not overstate identity.
2. Every experience claim has an evidence tier and a source.
3. Personal actions remain distinct from team outcomes.
4. Numbers retain their original definition, period, and scope.
5. Each capability has observable support in the preceding case.
6. Company claims use current primary sources with publication dates.
7. The answer adds judgment and relevance instead of repeating resume lines.
8. The spoken version fits the requested time at a natural pace.
9. The candidate can answer follow-ups on role, action, decision, difficulty,
   result, metric, and reflection.
10. Unsupported details are labelled for confirmation and excluded from the
    final spoken script.

## 7. Output Format

Return:

1. Positioning logic in one sentence.
2. Evidence map from each spoken claim to its source and tier.
3. Core self-introduction at the requested duration.
4. Short fallback version.
5. Optional follow-up modules.
6. Timing result from rehearsal or an explicit note that timing still needs a
   live read-through.
7. Missing facts and the smallest confirmation questions needed.
