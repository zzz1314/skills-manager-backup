# Interview Operating System

Read this reference for interview preparation, resume-to-question mapping,
mock interviews, and post-interview review.

## 1. Source Audit

Classify every input before using it:

1. Personal fact: a verified experience, action, result, or reflection.
2. Target-role fact: a current JD, official company source, or confirmed
   recruiter communication.
3. General method: an answer framework, review template, or practice method.
4. External lead: a slide deck, social post, anonymous interview report,
   copied prompt, or model example.

External leads can suggest questions and methods. They cannot prove personal
experience, company practice, interviewer intent, hiring standards, or success
rates. Flag unsupported percentages, time estimates, universal claims, and
marketing-style promises before reusing the material.

## 2. Resume-to-Question Map

For each resume line likely to attract attention, record:

| Field | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Resume statement | Preserve the current wording and version |
| Evidence tier | A, B, C, or D from the main skill |
| Intended role value | The capability or work scene it supports |
| Likely follow-ups | Scope, role, decision, action, difficulty, result, proof |
| Best supporting case | One verified experience card |
| Risk | Ambiguous ownership, weak metric, timeline conflict, or missing proof |
| Revision | Keep, clarify, compress, move, or remove after user approval |

Use high-information wording only when the user can defend the omitted detail
under follow-up. Do not manufacture curiosity through vague seniority,
inflated scope, or deliberately misleading omissions.

## 3. Experience Material Cards

Build a small set of high-coverage cards from verified experiences. Do not set
a universal target count or claim a fixed coverage percentage.

Each card should contain:

1. One-sentence context and the user's role.
2. The actual goal and success condition.
3. The key difficulty, conflict, or constraint.
4. The user's observations and judgment.
5. Two to four personal actions in sequence.
6. The result, metric definition, source, and contribution boundary.
7. A concrete reflection or next-time adjustment.
8. Questions the card can answer.
9. Facts that remain unconfirmed.

Prepare three depths without forcing exact speaking times:

1. Conclusion: direct answer and one proof point.
2. Core case: enough context, action, result, and reflection to stand alone.
3. Follow-up modules: metric definition, difficult decision, disagreement,
   failure, evidence, and alternative action.

Practice reconstruction from keywords. Avoid memorizing a full script word for
word. Stable facts and logic matter more than identical wording.

## 4. Question Interpretation

Use this sequence:

1. Answer the literal question directly.
2. List one or two plausible concerns being tested.
3. Select the concern most consistent with the role and conversation.
4. State the relevant judgment in plain language.
5. Support it with a verified case or a bounded scenario response.
6. Ask for clarification when materially different interpretations remain.

Possible concerns include motivation, stability, judgment, customer awareness,
role understanding, self-management, learning speed, collaboration, or risk
awareness. These are hypotheses until supported by context or follow-up.

Questions about socializing, overtime, travel, alcohol, or flexible schedules
require special care. Clarify the actual work requirement, state lawful and
professional boundaries, and discuss reliable relationship-building or time
management methods. Do not normalize unsafe drinking, personal favors, or
unlimited availability as a job requirement.

## 5. Mock Interview Design

Run practice in layers:

1. Resume verification: dates, role, ownership, metrics, and proof.
2. Behavioral depth: judgment, action sequence, disagreement, failure, and
   reflection.
3. Role scenarios: realistic decisions with authority and compliance limits.
4. Motivation and fit: evidence-backed reasons for the role and company.
5. Interruptions and compression: answer first, then expand on request.

After each answer, test the weakest claim. Do not reward fluent wording when
the evidence or reasoning is weak.

## 6. Review at Three Levels

### Single Interview

Record within 24 hours when possible:

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Question | As close to the original wording as possible |
| Original answer | Key sentences and order |
| Follow-up | What the interviewer asked next |
| Observable response | Interruption, note-taking, clarification, topic change |
| Interpretation | One or more hypotheses, clearly labelled |
| Weak point | Fact, structure, role understanding, research, or delivery |
| Revision | One targeted change |
| Test | A question that can expose the same weakness again |

Keep observations separate from interpretations. Interview length, warmth,
note-taking, detailed follow-up, and start-date questions are weak signals on
their own.

### Pattern Review

After several comparable interviews, often three to five, compare:

1. Repeated questions and repeated follow-up points.
2. Where answers repeatedly lose clarity or evidence.
3. Which experience cards are overused or underdeveloped.
4. Differences by role, industry, and interview stage.
5. Actual outcomes once known.

Do not merge incomparable roles or companies merely to increase the sample.

### Stage Review

At a meaningful recruiting checkpoint, review:

1. Target-role choice and application allocation.
2. Resume evidence quality and positioning.
3. Missing capabilities, knowledge, or proof.
4. Changes supported by repeated evidence.

Do not use one rejection or one positive conversation to make a major strategy
change.

## 7. Funnel Diagnosis

For each stage, record the count entering the stage and the count advancing:

`stage rate = advanced applications / comparable applications entering stage`

Always attach:

1. Time window.
2. Role and company group.
3. Numerator and denominator.
4. Withdrawn, pending, duplicate, and incomplete applications.
5. Known process differences.

Use the funnel to locate a diagnostic area:

1. Application to interview: target selection, resume evidence, JD mapping,
   timing, location, or hard constraints.
2. Early interview to later interview: experience depth, communication,
   motivation, role understanding, or basic fit.
3. Final interview to offer: comparative fit, headcount, compensation,
   location, timing, senior judgment, or factors that may remain unobservable.

Do not assign a cause from the rate alone. Combine the rate with question
records, feedback, source quality, and comparable cases.

## 8. Durable Updates

After a useful interview, update only the affected assets:

1. Question bank: add the original question and tested concern.
2. Experience card: add a verified detail, sharper boundary, or better
   reflection.
3. Role record: add company-specific facts and current process notes.
4. Method file: update only when the lesson generalizes across cases.

Preserve the previous version or original answer so later changes can be
evaluated against evidence.
